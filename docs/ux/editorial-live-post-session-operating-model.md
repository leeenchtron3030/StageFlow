# StageFlow Editorial Live vs Post-Session Operating Model
**Status:** Draft v0.1
**Source fidelity:** Exact chat draft.

> **Repository interpretation:** This exact Draft v0.1 records the primary current
> UX operating assumption: a small Editorial team covers multiple concurrent Sessions.
> Dedicated Stage editors remain compatible, and no staffing or workflow runtime is
> implemented by this document.

## 1. Core Operating Assumption

StageFlow should initially optimize for a **small Editorial team covering multiple concurrent Sessions**, not assume a dedicated editor per Stage.

Dedicated Stage editor workflows remain compatible.

## 2. Why This Matters

Multi-stage Events may exceed available Editorial staffing. StageFlow should let Editorial work from structured signals rather than continuous monitoring.

## 3. One Workflow, Two Operating Modes

- Live Triage: identify important material quickly enough for near-live use.
- Post-Session Review: systematically understand and complete review.

Same Candidate/Session workflow.

## 4. Live Editorial Mental Model

**What should I look at next?**

StageFlow prioritizes via Producer marks, Candidate strength, Session priority, intelligence recency, downstream urgency, backlog.

## 5. Post-Session Mental Model

**What happened here that we should preserve or use?**

Context depth rises; urgency falls.

## 6. Live Editorial Home

Editorial Event Queue / Live Triage, not manually opened Stage feeds.

## 7. Post-Session Editorial Home

Emphasize unreviewed Candidates, deferred items, stable packages, package revision impacts, review completeness.

## 8. Candidate Arrival

Enter queue without stealing focus.

## 9. Producer Mark Behavior

High-value human signal, not mandatory interruption.

Raise priority, show alert, preserve current context.

## 10. Live Candidate Review Depth

Fast context → approve/reject/defer → next.

Do not require metadata, frame-perfect trimming, taxonomy, final copywriting.

## 11. Live Approval

Accepts editorial value; may allow later boundary refinement.

## 12. Live Rejection

Fast and low-friction.

## 13. Live Deferral

Essential for more context, ongoing Session, lag, prioritization.

## 14. Live Candidate Range

Approximate range acceptable during triage if downstream policy permits.

## 15. Near-Live Output Path

Candidate → Editorial triage → approved Clip → lightweight Assembly/render → Marketing.

Preserve provenance, package basis, human approval, revision impact.

## 16. Session Package Not Yet Complete

Editorial may work against active/in-progress package, with later changes projected as impact.

## 17. Live Edge Versus Review Position

Being behind live is neutral unless Event goals make it consequential.

## 18. Editorial Capacity

Show priority queue, oldest wait, Sessions untouched, rate of Candidates/review. Avoid complex forecasting initially.

## 19. Falling Behind

Human workflow condition, not necessarily production incident.

## 20. Graceful Degradation

Overload preserves all Candidates; do not discard/auto-reject/auto-approve.

## 21. Session Ends

Transition naturally; no duplicate queue/lifecycle.

## 22. Package Stabilization

Presentation end does not necessarily mean deep-review-ready. Show package, transcript, Moment processing separately.

## 23. Post-Session Review Entry

When suitable, show package revision, Candidates, reviewed live, remaining.

## 24. Deep Review Workflow

1. Understand Session structure.
2. Producer marks.
3. Strong unreviewed.
4. Deferred.
5. Browse transcript/timeline for missed opportunities.
6. Refine ranges.
7. Confirm review status.

## 25. Candidate Discovery Beyond AI

Editorial can create Moments the automation missed.

## 26. Transcript-First Deep Review

Search/scan transcript, seek media, select text to create Candidate.

## 27. Media-First Deep Review

Visual/audio moments may not be text-reducible.

## 28. Candidate Clusters

Group temporal signals visually; durable merge semantics deferred.

## 29. Editorial Review Complete

Separate future authority from Production/Assembly/Render/Marketing completion.

## 30. Reopening Editorial Review

Later changes preserve historical completion and create new current review condition.

## 31. Event Close

Capture completion does not imply Editorial completion.

## 32. Post-Event Mode

Same application; urgency disappears; backlog processing may continue.

## 33. Dedicated Stage Editor Compatibility

Allow Stage filter/assignment as optimization, not architecture.

## 34. Specialist Editorial Roles

Possible later: live clip editor, transcript reviewer, social editor, lead, Stage reviewer. Do not require in v1.

## 35. Important Operational Distinction

Never conflate:

- media delay
- intelligence delay
- Editorial delay

## 36. Producer Visibility

Producer may see compact Editorial workflow status but it should not automatically create Producer Attention.

## 37. Marketing Visibility

Marketing sees approved Clips, not raw Editorial backlog.

## 38. Live Mode Success Test

One editor can cover several Stages by prioritized signals, review behind live, act rapidly, and distinguish human vs intelligence backlog.

## 39. Post-Session Success Test

Thorough review without linear full-session watching, manual file/timestamp matching, duplicated decisions, or lifecycle confusion.

## 40. Product Principle

During Event: StageFlow brings likely value to Editorial.
After Session: StageFlow provides enough context to find what automation missed.
Human judgment remains final authority over editorial value.
